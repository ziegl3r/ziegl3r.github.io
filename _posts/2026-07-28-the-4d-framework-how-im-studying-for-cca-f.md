Working through Anthropic's AI Fluency Framework Foundations course this week, and the piece I keep coming back to is the 4D Framework. Four Ds, no more, no less — worth pinning down exactly.

<!--more-->

## The four Ds

1. **Delegation** — deciding what to hand off to Claude versus what stays with a human. Not "can it do this" but "should it, and how much of it."
2. **Description** — how well you can specify the task: context, constraints, the shape of a good answer. Vague input reliably produces vague output.
3. **Discernment** — evaluating what comes back. Reading Claude's output critically instead of accepting it at face value, especially anywhere correctness compounds.
4. **Diligence** — the follow-through: verifying, testing, taking responsibility for the final result. The framework is explicit that the human doesn't get to hand off accountability along with the task.

## Where it shows up in practice

This maps directly onto how a Claude Code session actually goes. Delegation is deciding whether a refactor is a one-shot prompt or something I want to pair through step by step. Description is the difference between "clean this up" and a prompt that actually states the constraints. Discernment is reading the diff before accepting it. Diligence is running the tests instead of assuming green.

Treating these as four separate checkpoints — rather than one fuzzy "did the AI help" judgment — is the part of the framework I think will actually stick after the exam is over.
